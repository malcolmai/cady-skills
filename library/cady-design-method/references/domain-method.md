### Basis

#### Sources

- https://www.svpg.com/four-big-risks/
- https://www.designcouncil.org.uk/resources/the-double-diamond/
- **Completion rule:** Deliver the requested design or artifact with relevant evidence, limitations and next action. For journey work, include the material customer decision and coherent path. Retain material customer-decision context as concise narrative rather than requiring dedicated fields. Include only relevant surface detail; do not expose the method as a questionnaire or rigid format.
- **Composition rule:** Establish the accepted baseline and requested change. Preserve unaffected identity, controls, graphics and useful copy in delegated prompts and rendered review. Options vary the requested decision. User selections and company visual rules outrank method defaults. Standardise navigation, headers, controls and state within each surface family; let the core flow choose different layouts within one system. Across surfaces, share identity, colour roles and terminology with purpose-specific layout, density and typography. Check names, state and next action across handoffs; branding adds no permissions or workflow. Keep rationale and review metadata out of customer artifacts unless useful to their decision. Use hierarchy, spacing, contrast and real previews rather than redundant metadata. Apply relevant state and accessibility checks.

- **Exploration rule:** For material product, brand or creative re-imaginations, use three meaningfully different options as a soft default, adapting freely to the user prompt and task. The user's natural-language guidance takes precedence; no fixed option count or range. For an already selected direction or routine refinement, implement without reopening selection.

### Iteration

- **Handoff:** Give Engineering the trigger-to-result journey, material state/page changes, invariants, acceptance and source-owned design reference. Distinguish intended, mocked and observed behavior. Give QA the independent expectations, including fine controls and accessibility. A canvas, screenshot, physical prototype or manual walkthrough can supply the evidence.
- **Iterate:** Inspect the actual result against the reference; name the largest observed gap and make a focused change while preserving what works. Revisit intent when evidence contradicts it. End when the agreed experience is evidenced, with unresolved uncertainty explicit.
- **Prompt:** Use the current outcome, evidence and relevant method to identify the hardest user decision. In discovery, compare the current alternative and test the uncertain promise before polishing. When scaling, preserve working journeys and test changed needs. Recommend the smallest complete experience with clear action, feedback and recovery.
- **Principle:** Design starts with the customer decision and complete outcome. Customer psychology is an evidence-bounded lens inside customer-centred design, not a questionnaire or second framework. Apply relevant baseline areas; standalone artwork need not have a conversion goal. Surface overlays tune guidance only; they never create a role mode, prescribe a canvas or limit tools. Support informed choice; never manufacture urgency or conceal material trade-offs.
- **Reference rule:** Preserve required templates, identity masters and factual content. For inspiration, extract why hierarchy, rhythm, typography, imagery and motion work and synthesise an owned direction.

### Risk lens

- **Feasibility:** Check the hardest delivery dependency against available skills, technology, time and budget.
- **Usability:** Can representative users understand and complete the journey with appropriately calibrated trust?
- **Value:** Would the customer change from their current alternative? Interest alone is weak evidence.
- **Viability:** Assess cost, distribution and obligations; distinguish estimates from observed business results.
- **Selection rule:** Combine product/interface lenses, not exclusive categories or new offerings. QA delivery_types are assurance lenses; topics select methods. Use expertise; continue independent work.
