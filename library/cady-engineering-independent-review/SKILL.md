# Independent review

**Point:** A reviewer who did not produce the work checks the actual diff and evidence, so approval means it holds.

**Company context:** `cady_start {"task": "<what you are about to do, in one sentence>"}`

Independently review a diff, artifact or decision record: rerun its checks, rank findings and return land, fix or redesign. Use when: a change needs review, before merge or in a later batch, or a decision others will build on before it is acted on. Not for: root-causing a bug or triaging a bug report.

## Bets

- Report only what answers two questions of the change: is there a simpler yet effective way, and is it a real defect or an uncovered gap (not trivial or already covered). Instead of: A broad audit that reports every observation with equal weight.
- Step back to the relevant point(s) and how the change fits them before reviewing the diff. Instead of: Review the diff alone against its own acceptance.
- Start from the brief, acceptance and actual diff at named SHAs; read the producer's reasoning only after your own findings are written. Instead of: Read the producer's summary first and verify its claims.
- Treat unverified evidence as unrun, and rerun only to probe a suspected risk; cached passes and the landing checks cover the rest. Instead of: Accept the producer's report that the tests pass.
- Rank by consequence and look for authority, isolation and data-loss holes before style. Instead of: Comment on everything visible in the diff, style included, with equal weight.
- Read beyond the changed lines to find and to prove or refute findings: callers, other writers of the same data, and missing tests, migrations or entry points. Instead of: Review only the lines in the diff.
- Review asks whether a change adds anything the subject's level puts under not now. Instead of: Leaving the level to the producer alone.
- Match review to the kind of work: for checkable work, one plan review first and one change review in a batch after landing, covering a review-first change first; no wording rounds. Instead of: Cap review at two rounds, give every guidance change a separate writing review, and hold every change in the queue until its own review covers it.

## Independent review

- **By kind of work:** Checkable work (code, and any statement that is true or false of the code, such as a product map line) gets a change review of the diff, a plan review before building when it crosses map subjects or touches a review-first path, and always one batch review after landing of everything since the last one, its findings going on the follow-up list; a review-first change (schema, migrations, authority, security or isolation code) is covered first in that batch. A finding is a fact with evidence; the writer fixes it and the reviewer confirms only that fix. A fix round's confirmation is one bounded step that checks the named findings closed and the named checks ran on the reported commit; it never re-opens the full review, and anything genuinely new it finds goes back to the reviewer as a new finding. When the same kind of finding turns up a second time, stop: the cause is in how the work was produced (the wrong source, a missing check, an unclear task), so fix that and do not run another pass. Judgement work gets no review rounds: method and card wording, `AGENTS.md` prose and map phrasing are written once from the stated intent, and a reviewer raises only a statement that is false or one that contradicts another line an agent is served; a preference is not a finding. Judgement work is revised when a pattern shows across real runs (the transcripts), not when a reviewer or a single eval case suggests a better sentence; key cases are optional evidence, not a gate, except for a clause that governs sends, spend, permissions or what a recipient sees, which takes them before it lands.

### Change classes

- **Authority and security:** Identity and tenant come from the verified credential, never from input; every query, list, count, export and cache key is scoped; access is denied by default and fails closed on error; inputs are validated at the boundary; secrets and personal data stay out of logs and errors; external effects are idempotent or reconciled. Evidence: negative tests (another tenant, a missing permission, an expired or forged token) that fail without the change, and a read of every new entry point.
- **Data and migrations:** Shipped migrations are never edited; a change expands first and runs under both the old and the new code; drops and renames come later as their own contract step; backfills are bounded, resumable and idempotent; new query paths have indexes; long-running statements and full-table rewrites are avoided. Evidence: the migration applied to a copy from empty and from the last released state, with the resulting schema read back, and a stated rollback.
- **Every change:** Edge cases (empty, null, duplicate, very large, concurrent, retried), error paths, a parallel path that duplicates an existing owner (search the same nouns and verbs), dead code and stale docs. Does this add anything the subject's level (on the product map) puts under not now? When it serves a ready task: is each Done when line met, each non-goal left alone, and the diff within its size budget and owners?
- **Tests:** Tests assert the contract, not the implementation, without sleeps, order dependence or live provider calls; a deleted, skipped or loosened test carries its reason; a test changed only to pass, without a Done when line that changes what it asserts, is a blocker. For a regression or guard claim, show the relevant test fails without the fix in a scratch copy or separate worktree, never the reviewed tree. Report check results and the evidence for the claim.
- **Ui:** Loading, empty, error, denied and long-content states; keyboard operation, labels and focus; representative viewports; copy that says what happened and what to do next. Evidence: rendered captures of the changed states at representative viewports; code reading alone cannot prove a UI change. Accessibility depth follows `cady-devops-wcag-audit`.
- **Change review:** The change review, at the end, checks that the diff implements what the plan decided (the `Plan:` note, and the plan review where one ran), that each named risk and each follow-up the change claims is handled, and that nothing unplanned is added. Raise any real defect you see, but reopen the architecture only where the diff departs from the plan. A bug fix is checked against its stated cause and the places sharing it.
- **Checks:** Inspect prior review and check evidence for the claims it covers. Reuse a check only when its relevant content, baseline/head relationship, selection, toolchain and environment remain valid; a producer assertion alone is not proof. Serious findings, conflicts, or stale, missing or doubtful evidence invalidate affected conclusions: investigate, and rerun only to probe a suspected risk, at the reviewed head on a clean tree; the writer's cached passes and the landing checks cover the rest. A red that is red on main too is not a finding. Report commands, revisions and results; unverified evidence is unrun, not passed. Actual effect authority and live environment checks stay fresh. Read beyond changed lines to find and to prove or refute findings: callers, other writers of the same data and missing tests, migrations, docs or entry points. When a software brief names owner × quality cells, use them as lenses with `../cady/references/quality-classes.md`; otherwise use the change classes below. Those classes remain the evidence floor.
- **Findings:** Report only findings that are a real defect, an uncovered gap, or a simpler way that works as well; leave out what is trivial or already covered. Rank by consequence: a blocker (wrong behaviour; a security, isolation or authority defect; data-loss risk; a claimed check that does not hold) goes back to the writer, first on the follow-up list, or is reverted at once when it is a security, isolation or authority defect. A follow-up does not block: the verdict stays land, and it goes on the repository's follow-up list, or a task (work_set) where it has none. Where the repository has `followups.py`, one lane claims the open follow-ups (`claim --lane <branch>`) and lands them as one PR whose body names them (`Follow-ups: <ids>`); the batch reviewer takes the batch with `claim --review --lane <branch>` and records what it covered with `close --id batch-review --through <sha>`. Give each in one or two lines: file:line, the failure, a fix, and whether it is verified or suspected. No findings is a valid approval when the evidence supports it.
- **Independence:** Before reviewing, step back to the relevant point(s) (the map page's, the method's, the goal's) and how this change fits them; a minor map change (wording, a status, a row) needs no explicit review, a larger one or a plan does. Refuse a review of work you produced or watched being produced, and say so; the requester finds another reviewer. Start from the task's acceptance and the actual diff at named base and head SHAs; a review request carries only those, never the producer's claims, check results or verdict; read the producer’s reasoning only after your own findings are written. Review may build on sound independent coverage of an integrated baseline: inspect the new delta and affected dependencies and seams, so the named integrated head is covered without restarting unchanged review.
- **Levels:** Review at two levels. First why: does the work serve the purpose one level up? Ask why until you reach a recorded goal, bet or decision, and list each assumption with its evidence or mark it unsupported. A why that is missing goes back to the requester as a question. A why that evidence contradicts is a blocker even when the details are sound. A why that conflicts with an owner's recorded decision goes to that owner; you do not reverse it. Then review the details. An obviously purposeful small fix needs one line on why.
- **Plan review:** The plan review, before building a change that crosses map subjects or touches a review-first path, is its one architecture review: against the map subject's point and bets, decide the right owner and layer, the existing primitive to reuse, what the change removes, and that it holds rather than patches; then complete the risks to watch, naming every gap of a class at once.
- **Verdict:** End with land, fix (the blockers to clear) or redesign (why the approach is wrong and the alternative); the writer has already queued the change at hand-back; on fix or redesign, the findings go back to the writer, or to one replacement writer, and stop the change from landing until answered. Post ranked findings (prior coverage reused, checks and results) as one PR comment ending in its record block (verdict, reviewed SHA, reviewer, producer), with `review_comment.py post` where the repository has it (`--help` lists its options and which later heads a verdict covers), or return it to the requester to post verbatim; it is the one record of the review, and the producer never edits it. On a review-first change, or one of about 400 changed lines or more, the comment opens with a tl;dr for the owner of at most five short lines: what it fixes and the goal it serves, tradeoffs that reach across the product and why each lasts, risk, and the one question for the owner if there is one. Review later fixes and affected seams as the integrated state changes.
