# Engineering cycle dimension register and research

**Point:** Name MECE dimensions with one owner, question and label each, and research only the differentiating ones.

- **Register:** 3. Name high-level, mutually exclusive, collectively exhaustive dimensions that every goal crosses, each with one question, owner and boundary. Before users or revenue, name only the 3 to 5 a goal actually crosses. With users, expect 8 to 14. For a product you build in code, keep folded owners as one-line homes; every code area has exactly one owner, including a module’s SQL, pages and tests. Do not register qualities such as cost, isolation, verification or efficiency as owners; use `quality-classes.md`. For other products, choose dimensions from how the offering is made, delivered and used; fold inactive dimensions into the active owners as decisions. Scope, ICP and pricing live in direction and decisions, not the register. Keep only what has its own owner, question and test; add one only for a new subsystem, region, provider or buyer category. Draw the map as the product card's map recipe says. Where applicable, keep authentication separate from permissions, agent action authority separate from user access, and agent management under its own owner. Name the commercial category a buyer would compare. Label each differentiating or not on the map's Dimensions page, with its reason. Only a real choice (a binding rule, or a standard-tool choice such as "use Stripe plainly") is recorded (a bet at the product node with decisions_set, or a test or check for a rule); raise it to Product as an ask (asks_set) with your recommendation, and never record a draft label.
- **Research:** 4. Depth follows the label. A non-differentiating dimension takes the most reliable widely used approach with the least custom code; deviation needs a named reason. A differentiating dimension gets a reviewed architecture; apply the Product card’s depth judgement on the decisive problem from the first session, including before users or revenue. Add recurring weekly scans only after the first users or revenue; before then research only where that judgement calls for it. Research names the problem space, who does the best work in it now and their latest published method, and maps it to the dimension's goal as concise do and don't guidance; the gap is the current design against that. Group differentiating dimensions N to 1 into research tracks where their commercial solutions are linked, and scan each track weekly with `cady-product-research` (its technology and competition references) for material improvements since the last scan; keep the scan as a File cited from the track's work item, and test any promising idea with `cady-company-experiment` before it changes the architecture.
