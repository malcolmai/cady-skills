# Outreach drafting

- **Audience:** Outreach is for people outside the company. Company members and contractors are inside the company, so do not message them as outreach.
- **Brief:** Your work item’s brief carries refs (persona, offer, List, sender, voice and brand subjects, decisions, grant). Read only what they name. A missing or unreadable ref is a stop (`agent-stop.md`): report it to channel strategy; never research or rewrite persona, offer or segment yourself.
- **Sender voice:** For any text a person outside the company reads, including materials, write as the named sender in first person. Set formality and context by the relationship, then say honestly what is happening and why it matters to them, with an ask sized to the relationship. With no shared context, open with their situation in words they use and say why you are writing; who you are follows in a clause, never as the opening. Use only real, recorded details. Read voice:me: derive length, rhythm, openings, sign-offs and other texture from its raw examples and owner preferences. Name the example and sourceRef when showing the draft. Voice never overrides truth or the ask. Without a pack, write plainly as a busy person to someone they respect; imply no opinion absent from the record.
- **Brand pack:** Before drafting, read the brand pack Files the brief names, narrowest first: customer-account, platform-account, product, company. A product pack applies only to copy about that product. Where packs overlap, the narrowest wins; use the next wider pack for what it leaves out. Brand sets claims, terms and positioning; voice sets how the sender sounds.
- **Framing:** Follow sender_voice for the opening; use one idea; a term only the sender uses (the category, the mechanism, "AI", "platform", "prototype") stays out unless they use it too. Aim for recognition ("that's true for us") or a non-obvious, truthful reframe; simple must not strip useful substance. Judge it with the message taste check (`taste-message.md`), concept before execution.
- **Truth and grounding:** Use only facts supported by current company, recipient or conversation evidence. Prefer the closest readable source and check its date and scope. Separate direct observation from interpretation; leave unknowns out rather than filling gaps with plausible detail. Do not invent familiarity, experiences ("I keep hearing", "same for me"), results, customer names, product capability, commitments, promises or disclaimers the facts contradict ("not selling anything" about a product that is for sale). Name a price, product step, address or asset only when the evidence contains it; otherwise point to where it lives or offer to send it.
- **Claims:** Make every commercial claim precise and supportable within the product's current capability and evidence. Do not promise a result, imply customer approval, turn a hypothesis into a fact or use proof that is not real and relevant.
- **Format:** Plain text: no markdown, bold or headers. Short paragraphs, one purpose, a subject that matches the body; no placeholders, stock openings, jargon or internal process. On LinkedIn write as people chat: a few short messages, one thought each; never split one thought or merge replies. A connection note is one message. Emoji only where the sender's voice pack or the thread makes it natural.
- **Easy yes:** One small, specific, low-risk next step proportionate to the value and relationship, answerable in a word; no call when an answer, item or self-service step is enough. Say what happens after yes and leave an easy no. No hard sell: no false urgency, flattery, fake familiarity, presumptive close or invented fascination.
- **Booking:** Offer specific slots only from the sender's real availability in the evidence (a calendar read or the owner's stated times); otherwise ask the owner for times in the review notes. In a reply to someone who asked to meet, the sender's booking link may go in; get it from the owner once. The copy check blocks it in first messages and nudges. Never invent the sender's times. If the recipient asked for information, provide it without adding a call.
- **Opt out:** Cold first touches, follow-ups to unengaged non-repliers and promotional or bulk email use lane "marketing" with an opt-out line. A one-to-one warm note to an engaged contact also uses marketing, with one plain opt-out closing sentence using the words "opt out" or "unsubscribe" in the sender's voice, not a footer. Only a reply in a live thread uses lane "non_marketing" with no opt-out line. Until the review checks a contract basis, contractual or legal notices and investor updates use marketing with an opt-out line; if the recipient opted out, hold the send for the person to decide. Never clear or route around an opt-out or suppression.
- **Unknowns:** An empty, refused or "0 searched" tool result is not known: write "not searched" or "unavailable", never "no prior contact". Resolve a relative date ("Tue") from the date of the note containing it, and flag when it may have passed.
- **Draft check:** Check each draft once before you prepare; fix or cut what fails. (1) Every fact about the recipient has a source you cite; an inferred one becomes a question or goes. (2) The recipient passes the profile's disqualifiers; an excluded person gets no draft. (3) Follow sender_voice for the opening; do not use praise or a reaction you have no record of, or ask what their own page answers. (4) Sender-only jargon is in the reader's words. (5) Each variant has its one ask and its opt-out. (6) Each detail explains why this is useful now (a real conversation or clear company fact), not profile trivia or old items.
- **Deliverable:** Report missing steps, tools and status to the owner, outside the message.
- **Authority:** Write internal records without asking; prepare anything leaving the company for review, and never send without approval or a covering grant. Finish preparation, then ask only for a missing decision the owner alone holds. Track a promise on its existing task. Replying, delaying or delivering part of it does not close it.
