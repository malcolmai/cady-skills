# Taste check

- **Check:** You cannot reliably perceive how a person will take your work, and you favour your own style. Route the judgment; rereading your own work is not judging it.

1. Before making it: name the reader, what the work must get them to do, and 2 to 4 yes/no reader tests a stranger could answer (can they tell it is AI? is it theirs? do they get it at a glance?). A number is a test only if it names the reader's failure. If the concept is open, make two or three different candidates.
2. Tools first: run every check in your medium part and fix what they find.
3. As consumed: judge the rendered page, the email as received, the frame at delivery size. Otherwise write "not inspected in medium" and claim no quality.
4. Fresh judge, staged: a separate helper (another model family where available) gets only the artifact and a one-line reader. It records its first impression and answers the tests in the reader's words; only then does it see the owner's taste decisions and the tells. Your reasoning comes after its verdict.
5. Verdict: concept first, then structure, then detail, against the current version: "A over B for this reader because ..., giving up ...". Keeping it as it is is a valid verdict; a close call is a tie.
6. The person decides what a model cannot (tone, persuasion, humour, fine visual preference) and anything consequential to a named person or the public: show two unlabelled real alternatives, once. Real responses outrank every judge.
7. Record an owner correction as a quoted, scoped taste:<medium>-<key> decision (submit_company_work_checkpoint); it is the next judge's reference.

## Investor

- Tools: reconcile every number to a dated source, and keep metric definitions identical across deck, model and data room.
- Tests for a partner seeing it cold: in two minutes, what does the company do, what is the evidence, what is the ask? Judge slides rendered as slides.
- Judge: the fresh judge gets only the material and "a seed partner, cold". The founder approves; investor replies are the signal.

## Message

- Tools: the outreach copy check (placeholders, opt-out, forbidden openers, the company's caps, tells as warnings).
- Tests: can they understand the actual purpose and reasonably respond (the recipient test)? Does it read as this sender typing to this person? Is the reason real and theirs? Can they answer in a word?
- Judge: the message, the relationship stage and two of the sender's voice examples; it picks which of two drafts the sender wrote. The outreach review is the person's own read; reply rates are the signal.

## Post

- Tools: the tells scan; check every link and claim against its source.
- Tests: would this audience stop for the idea without the author's name? What one thing would they repeat?
- Judge in placement: the feed preview and the first two lines before the fold. For hooks and headlines, models shortlist and the owner picks. Engagement is a signal, not proof of quality.

## Tells

- Patterns that mark text as AI-written. A tell is a pointer, not proof: a person's own verified older messages win, so if they typed dashes before 2023 a dash is theirs. The outreach copy check scans the listable ones (characters, openers, hedges, trend words, markdown) as warnings; structure and uniformity need a judge.
- Characters: an em dash in a chat message; curly quotes in a channel where the person types straight ones; an ellipsis character where they type three dots.
- Openers and closers (quote exactly): "I hope this finds you well", "hope you're doing well", "looking forward to hearing", "Let me know if you have any questions", "Great question", "Absolutely!".
- Eager hedges and diminished asks: "happy to", "feel free to", "no worries if not", "quick chat", "quick call", "pick your brain".
- Self-introduction before relevance: a self-introduction or company pitch as the opening paragraph, ahead of the reason it matters to them. A one-clause honest reason is not a tell.
- Trend words: delve, leverage, synergy, unlock, streamline, elevate, foster, crucial, seamless, robust, "excited to", thrilled.
- Structure: a group of three, "not X but Y", "it's not just X", a closing summary line, a rhetorical question, bold or bullets in a chat.
- Two or more of these together: uniform sentence length with perfect punctuation in a chat, curly quotes, trend words.

## Ui

- Tools: render each changed state at desktop and narrow widths; run the accessibility and keyboard pass (cady-design-wcag-audit); check contrast, overflow and clipping, the project's lints, and a downscaled render for hierarchy. Code cannot prove a UI.
- Judge: a vision-capable fresh judge writes its first impression before it sees the flow goal, then compares. Walk the journeys blind (cady-qa-stories). Close visual choices go to the owner as rendered pairs.

## Visual

- Tools: a contact sheet for a set and one frame per beat at delivery size; text legible, nothing clipped, captions present, real interface pixels unaltered.
- Judge: the fresh judge looks or watches before it reads the script and says what the piece is about and what it wants the viewer to do. Pacing, humour and feel are the person's calls.
