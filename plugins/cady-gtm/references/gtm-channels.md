# GTM channel comparison

- **Choice:** Compare channels on who they reach, cost and time to a signal, so the mix fits the persona and stage. Rank first by where the persona already looks and whom they trust (the persona File's how-they-buy section), then by time to first signal against the goal date, the cost the stage can carry (money, founder hours, reputation or account risk), and learning speed. A channel the persona does not use loses however cheap it is. With a dated goal still at zero, lead with channels needing no new setup or grant that can signal inside the window; start slow-setup channels in parallel only when cheap and not competing for the same founder hours.
- **Experiment:** Run one or two channels at a time, each with its own pre-registered test (`cady-company-experiment`), stop signal and review date. Size each test before it runs at about 20 exposures per arm, with pass and fail lines set on that sample; a smaller test is directional and decides nothing. Scale only a channel that moves the goal's own measure, such as qualified calls, never replies, reach or sign-ups alone. Record the mix as one function-head decision with alternatives, what would reverse it, and a reviewBy date.
- **Boundary:** Name the angle and the customer's words it rests on; the channel's execution work drafts the copy. Write no message copy, subject line, post text or send step, even when asked what would be sent: copy here skips the drafting check and review and fixes text before the brief exists. Channel strategy sets send dates, cadence, caps, sequences, variants and briefs from the channel rules.
- **Cold email:** Fit: buyers with findable work email and a shared sharp reason; useful for an early test once the sender is ready. Pros: volume and clean variant tests. Cons: low reply rates; sender setup takes time. Cost: low money, setup hours and domain reputation risk. First signal: after sender warm-up. Learning: fast once each variant has about 20 exposures. Stop signal: stops under `agent-stop.md`; warm-up per `cold-deliverability.md`; or no positive reply after two cycles of about 20 exposures per variant. Execution: `cady-gtm-cold-email-cycle`.
- **Warm network:** Fit: founder knows buyers or is one introduction away, especially for an early signal. Pros: trust and strong reply rates, with no setup. Cons: finite, biased sample; pitching spends goodwill. Cost: founder hours and relationship reputation. First signal: days, often within a week. Learning: slow to generalise. Stop signal: network exhausted, or polite replies without calls. Execution: `cady-gtm-warm-email`.
- **Linkedin:** Fit: buyers active there and a sender profile credible to them; useful early at low volume. Pros: personal and cheap. Cons: weekly limits and possible account restriction or ban. Cost: founder hours and account risk. First signal: days. Learning: medium, limited by volume. Stop signal: any challenge or restriction notice, or acceptances without replies. Execution: `cady-gtm-linkedin-message`.
- **Community:** Fit: buyers gather in a forum or group where helping is allowed; early discovery can reveal their language. Pros: trust and qualitative insight. Cons: promotion may be forbidden and trust takes time. Cost: founder hours and reputation risk. First signal: one to three weeks. Learning: rich but qualitative. Stop signal: moderator warning, or no questions or DMs after weeks of contribution. Execution: no dedicated skill yet; follow `taste-post.md` and the posting skill when it exists.
- **Events:** Fit: buyers attend a specific event or deals are high value; useful when its date fits the goal. Pros: deep conversations and fast trust. Cons: fixed dates, few events and travel. Cost: money and founder days. First signal: event date. Learning: slow. Stop signal: no qualified follow-up from two events. Execution: `cady-gtm-events`.
- **Content:** Fit: buyers search for the problem or follow voices on it; suits a longer horizon. Pros: compounds and brings inbound. Cons: slow and needs distribution. Cost: founder hours. First signal: weeks to months. Learning: slow. Stop signal: no qualified response after about four weeks. Execution: no dedicated posting skill yet.
- **Launches:** Fit: a one-day attention spike can reach the persona, at a suitable launch stage. Pros: burst of sign-ups. Cons: one shot and the audience may not be the ICP. Cost: preparation hours and reputation. First signal: the day itself. Learning: one sample. Stop signal: sign-ups outside the ICP. Execution: no dedicated execution skill; cady-gtm-launch coordinates a launch.
- **Paid:** Fit: demand exists and the path to the goal's measure is proven. Pros: fast and controllable. Cons: spends cash; before product-market fit it is only a capped test within a Finance ceiling. Cost: money and setup hours. First signal: days. Learning: fast with budget. Stop signal: the pre-registered cost or payback rule fires. Execution: no dedicated skill yet; consult the marketing card.
